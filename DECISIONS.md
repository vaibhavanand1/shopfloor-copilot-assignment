# Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE) Decision Rationale
**Project:** ShopFloor Copilot — AI-Assisted Visual Inspection & SOP Agent  
**Author:** Forward-Deployed Engineer  
**Vertical:** Manufacturing (CNC Machining & Plastics Injection)

---

### Section G: Architectural & Operational Decision Prompts

#### 1. Why Vector RAG (vs. a vectorless/long-context read) fits this SOP corpus — and what would change your answer if the plant's SOP set were fixed at 20 pages instead of continuously growing.
In an industrial precision plant, SOP corpora are living documents that expand continuously with new CNC tooling updates, Engineering Change Notices (ECNs), customer quality agreements, and shift handover bulletins. Vector RAG enables modular, sub-millisecond updates where an updated or added SOP section can be indexed incrementally without re-prompting or expanding the model's context window. Shoving ever-growing documentation into a long-context window introduces severe latency penalties on local edge hardware (often 3–5x slower Time-to-First-Token), increases attention dilution ("needle in a haystack" failures where specific numerical tolerances like 0.2 mm get overlooked), and drastically spikes VRAM usage. Conversely, if the plant's documentation were strictly frozen at 20 static pages (~8,000 tokens), an optimized long-context or KV-cached prompt architecture would become superior: it would eliminate chunking boundary fragmentation, preserve holistic document context across disparate sections, and completely avoid vector store infrastructure complexity while fitting easily inside local edge LLM context buffers.

#### 2. Why an agent loop (vs. a fixed pipeline) is justified for the tool-selection step here — and name the one part of this system you would NOT make agentic, and why.
An agentic ReAct loop is justified because shop floor queries exhibit multimodal variability in input modalities, intent, and operational severity that cannot be captured cleanly by rigid if-else logic. A worker may submit a text-only procedural inquiry, an uncaptioned photo requiring autonomous CV classification and OCR extraction, or an urgent escalation requiring conditional cross-referencing between ERP traveler status and shift directory contacts. The agent dynamically decides tool sequencing (e.g., executing OCR first to extract a Work Order ID, which subsequently triggers an ERP lookup only when an ID exists). The single part of the system that must **NEVER** be made agentic is the terminal scrap and ERP status mutation gate (`propose_escalation_or_scrap`). On a live manufacturing floor, automating irreversible actions (marking parts as scrapped in the ERP, charging scrap expenses to job cost centers, or halting machine cycles) creates catastrophic operational and financial risks; this gate must strictly remain a deterministic, non-agentic proposal requiring authenticated physical human sign-off.

#### 3. Why you chose classical CV, a pretrained model, or both for the defect classifier — and what would push you to fine-tune a custom model instead.
We implemented classical OpenCV computer vision (contour solidity, aspect-ratio bounding box analysis, Gaussian blur, and Canny edge filtering) because precision CNC parts manufactured under controlled illumination exhibit predictable geometric and contrast signatures. Classical CV is 100% deterministic, runs in under 15 milliseconds on CPU without requiring GPU acceleration or downloading gigabytes of weights, and produces mathematically explainable metrics (e.g., solidity < 0.96 for sheared corners, edge pixel density for cracks) that can be audited by quality engineers. In contrast, deep vision models introduce latency and non-deterministic false positives under subtle specular reflections. We would immediately push to fine-tune a custom model (e.g., YOLOv8-seg or an autoencoder defect detector) if the manufacturing environment scaled to complex freeform injection-molded geometries, textured composite materials, or variable ambient lighting where deterministic edge thresholds break down, or if the defect taxonomy expanded to subtle metallurgical anomalies (e.g., porosity, subsurface inclusions, heat-treatment discoloration) that lack sharp edge gradients.

#### 4. If this pilot needed to scale from one plant to ten, which cost-optimization lever — prompt/response caching, model tiering, batching, a smaller fine-tuned model, or quantization — would you apply first, and why that one before the others.
When scaling across ten facilities, the first cost-optimization lever to apply is **model tiering (cascading)** paired with deterministic templating. On a shop floor, 60–70% of worker inquiries follow repetitive patterns (e.g., routine tolerance checks, station routing lookups, contact extensions) that can be resolved instantly by deterministic rules or an ultra-compact quantized model (e.g., 1B–3B SLM or static SOP templating) with near-zero compute cost, reserving heavier edge models (e.g., 8B–14B) strictly for ambiguous or multi-step anomalies. We choose tiering before quantization or fine-tuning because it slashes average inference load at the architectural level without compromising complex edge cases, avoids the heavy engineering and labeling overhead of fine-tuning ten distinct plant-specific models, and immediately respects local hardware constraints on standard plant edge PCs.

#### 5. Name one governance risk specific to a shop floor (not a generic AI risk) that this design does not yet fully address, and how you'd close it in a real engagement.
A shop-floor specific governance risk is **"Ghost Scrapping" / Traveler Desynchronization via Operator Over-Reliance (Automation Bias)**: an operator might assume the Copilot's visual classification is infallible, discard a salvageable part into the physical scrap bin without obtaining the mandated supervisor sign-off, or conversely route a cracked part into assembly because they misframed the photo and the CV didn't capture the fracture. In a real engagement, we would close this by implementing **Hardware-Interlocked Verification & Dual-Custody Digital Sign-Off**: the physical quarantine bin remains electronically locked via an IoT relay (e.g., PLC / GPIO interlock) that only unlocks when a certified Quality Supervisor scans their physical RFID badge and approves the Copilot's digital proposal on the terminal. Furthermore, we would enforce mandatory photographic capture from fixed telecentric camera fixtures (rather than unconstrained handheld phones) with camera calibration sanity checks (lighting, focus, part alignment) before any inspection inference runs.

### Section E: Contextual Bandit Action-Space Justification

#### Why Optimize `rag_top_k` vs. OCR Engine?
We chose to optimize **`rag_top_k`** over the OCR engine because `rag_top_k` represents a direct, continuous trade-off between **context comprehensiveness and execution latency** that directly impacts the worker's experience and answer correctness. 

1. **OCR Engine is an Operational Constraint, Not a Trade-off Variable:** In local manufacturing edge deployments, OCR engine selection is largely deterministic: `pytesseract` with the local binary is either present, ultra-fast (~15 ms), and accurate on stamped text, or it is absent and requires a fallback. Once a clear stamped label is cropped, OCR either parses the text or fails; varying the OCR engine dynamically does not provide a meaningful trade-off curve.
2. **`rag_top_k` Directly Governs Information Completeness vs. Token Latency:** In contrast, RAG retrieval volume directly controls the prompt payload, token processing latency, and the probability of capturing secondary cross-document rules. For simple tolerance lookups (`no_defect`, `surface_scratch`), a single chunk (`top_k=1`) is sufficient, and minimizing latency maximizes operator utility. For severe incidents (`crack`), a single chunk only provides tolerance definitions while omitting critical secondary contacts (Quality Lead Marcus Vance ext. 4402) and scrap traveler tags, causing worker dissatisfaction.
3. **Empirical Policy Shift:** By optimizing `rag_top_k` under the reward formula `Reward = (Score_feedback * 10) - Latency_seconds`, the bandit actively learns the optimal information density per defect state: driving simple inspections to `top_k=1` for sub-second responses while shifting complex defect states to `top_k=3` where the benefit of complete multi-document grounding outweighs the minor latency penalty.
